# Enterprise AI Field Guide

A practical guide to designing, building, and operating AI in the enterprise.

[Start reading](enterprise-ai-technology-stack.md) - [Explore the chapters](#explore-the-guide) - [Suggest a topic](../../issues/new)

## Why I'm Writing This

I like technology that helps people get things done. I also want to know who owns it, what it costs, and what happens when it breaks.

After more than 25 years in IT, I've learned that getting something working is only part of the job. Making it something the business can depend on takes engineering, judgment, and people willing to own the outcome. AI brings that familiar work into some unfamiliar territory.

This guide shares how I think through those decisions, including the tradeoffs, surprises, and lessons that come with doing the work. Expect practical explanations, real experiences, and room for the fact that decisions rarely arrive with perfect information.

## Find Your Starting Point

If you are responsible for bringing AI into an enterprise, this guide is for you. Engineers and architects will find the system connections behind the decisions. Technology leaders will find questions about ownership, cost, risk, and delivery that deserve attention before an experiment becomes a business dependency.

You do not need to be a data scientist. Familiarity with applications and infrastructure will help. Start with Part I for the foundation, or choose a chapter that speaks to the work in front of you.

### Where the Guide Goes Next

Future Parts will cover building the capabilities, proving and operating them, and making enterprise AI sustainable. That includes models, retrieval, tools, internal platforms, evaluation, reliability, disaster recovery, FinOps, and leadership.

This guide will grow as I publish new chapters and revisit existing material. AI capabilities, services, and practices change quickly, so check the linked sources for current details. My focus is on practical judgment that remains useful as the technology evolves.
